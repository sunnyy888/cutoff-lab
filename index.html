🏨 Cutoff Lab

A model gives every hotel booking a probability of being canceled. Where do you draw the line? Drag the cutoff and watch the confusion matrix, the costs and the ROC point move. Data: 119,390 real bookings from two Portuguese hotels (Antonio, Almeida & Nunes, 2019).

1. No peeking at the future: leakage and the time split

Columns we dropped (and one we kept), and why
Why it matters

The prediction is made when the booking comes in, so every feature must be known at that moment. Several columns in this dataset are only filled in at check-in or cancellation. They make a model look brilliant on paper and useless in practice.

The split is by arrival date, never shuffled: train on the past, choose the cutoff on validation, report on the future (test). Shuffling would let the model learn from bookings that happen after the ones it's scored on. The test months (summer 2017) also cancel more often than training, which is a realistic drift the model has to live with.

2. Pick a cutoff

Flag a booking as "will cancel" when its score ≥ cutoff, then overbook that room. False positive: you flagged it but the guest arrives, so you must walk a guest to another hotel (default $250). False negative: you missed a cancellation and the room sits empty (default $100 of lost revenue).

3. What the model leans on

Permutation importance: how much validation AUC drops when one column is shuffled (5 repeats; bars show ±1 SD).

⚠️ This is NOT causation

Importance means "the model uses this column to predict", not "changing this column would change cancellations". The top feature is a perfect example: of non-refundable bookings were canceled, against with no deposit. Making every rate non-refundable would not raise cancellations to 99%. In this dataset 97% of non-refundable bookings come from group blocks (63%) and offline travel agents (34%), booked a median of 183 days ahead (vs 57 for everyone else). Blocks like that get released in bulk. The column marks who is booking, not a lever.

To learn what a policy would do, run an experiment: randomly offer some guests a different deposit policy and compare cancellation rates.

Models trained by train.py (scikit-learn): logistic regression with one-hot categories and standardized numerics, and histogram gradient boosting. The page uses each model's score histogram (1,000 bins) on validation and test, so every number updates instantly in your browser.
